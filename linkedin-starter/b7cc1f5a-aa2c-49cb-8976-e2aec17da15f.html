Photo, Banner, and Open to Work

Photo: Use a photo with a plain or softly blurred background, natural light if possible, and a clean business-appropriate top. A direct, relaxed expression works better than a stiff pose. Shoulders-up framing is the standard for LinkedIn. Avoid busy backgrounds, heavy filters, or group crops. Banner: A clean, professional banner at 1584 x 396 pixels works best. Connecting and Building Content

Who to Connect With

Your network is the highest-leverage asset you have on LinkedIn. A strong profile only matters if the right people are seeing it.

Here are the categories of people to focus on. You don't need specific names yet. Use LinkedIn's search to find them in your target region.

Recruiters in your field. Search "recruiter" plus your profession (for example, "accounting recruiter" or "IT recruiter"). Filter by the city or region you want to work in. These are the people whose job is to move candidates like you.

Hiring managers at companies you'd like to work for. Think about the 10 to 20 companies that you'd say yes to immediately. Then find the person one level above the job you want. If you want a staff-accountant role, look for the controller or finance manager. If you want a coordinator role, look for the team lead or director.

Peers who hold the same credential you do. If you have a CPA, CAT, ACCA, PMP, RN, or any other professional designation, search for others with that same credential in your target country. Shared credentials are an instant warm opener.

Alumni from schools or programs you attended. LinkedIn's alumni tool lets you filter your school by city, industry, and graduation year. Alumni respond at much higher rates than cold contacts.

Immigration consultants (if you're on a PR or work-permit pathway). They talk to employers every day and often know who's looking before jobs get posted.

Always include a short note with your connection request. Never send it blank. Two or three sentences is enough. Say who you are, why you're reaching out, and what you're hoping for. Acceptance rates go up significantly when you add a note.

Starting to Build Content

You don't need to be a content creator. You just need your profile to look active when a recruiter checks it. Here's how to make that happen.

Add a photo. It doesn't have to be professional. A clean head-and-shoulders shot taken with your phone, in natural light, in front of a plain background, is fine. Profiles with photos get about 14 times more views than profiles without one.

Write a short summary. Your About section is the easiest thing to refresh. Even 3 or 4 paragraphs about what you do and what you're looking for is plenty. Keep it plain and direct.

Focus on your professional skill areas. Pin your top 3 skills at the top of your Skills section. LinkedIn's search engine weighs these heavily. Put the most important ones first.

Tag people you know when you post. If you write about a project, mention the colleague who worked on it with you. Tagging pulls your post into their network's feed too, which multiplies your reach for free.

Aim for about 10 connection requests a day. LinkedIn's "People You May Know" section on your home page is a goldmine. It surfaces people who are already one degree removed from you. Send 10 requests a day from that list, five days a week. Add a short note each time.

Post once a week to start. You don't need to post every day. One real post per week is enough to keep your profile looking active.

What kinds of posts work for job seekers:

  • A lesson you learned at work recently (one paragraph is fine)
  • A behind-the-scenes look at something you do well (walk through a typical day or task)
  • Progress on a credential or course you're working on
  • A short reaction to an industry article you read
  • Celebration of someone else's win in your field

Keep posts short. Three to five short paragraphs. Break lines often. White space is your friend.

What to avoid:

  • Generic motivational quotes (they don't get engagement)
  • Posts that only say "I'm looking for work" (post them occasionally, not weekly)
  • Copied posts (LinkedIn's algorithm suppresses duplicates)
  • Arguing in comment threads (it's a bad look for job seekers)

That's it. Photo, summary, skills pinned, 10 connection requests a day, one post a week. Keep it up for 60 days and your profile will look completely different to recruiters.
