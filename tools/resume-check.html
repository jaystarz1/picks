Is your resume Canadian-ready?

Ten yes-or-no questions against the conventions Canadian employers scan for. Instant score, nothing collected.

Answer honestly, with your current resume in front of you

These are the ten conventions Canadian recruiters and applicant tracking systems quietly screen for. A resume that worked well back home usually breaks several of them. That gap, not a lack of skill, is the most common reason strong candidates hear nothing back.

1. Is your resume free of a photo?
Canadian resumes never carry a photo. Employers here are trained to screen them out.
2. Is it free of your date of birth, marital status, and nationality?
Personal details that are normal on resumes elsewhere are red flags to Canadian HR.
3. Is it two pages or less?
One page early-career, two pages maximum for everyone else.
4. Does every role use bullet points that start with an action verb?
Paragraphs of duties get skimmed past. Led, built, reduced, delivered.
5. Do most bullets carry a number, a result, or a scale?
Team size, budget, percentage improved, volume handled. Numbers are what recruiters remember.
6. Do you tailor the resume for each application?
One generic resume for every posting is the single most common mistake.
7. Is the layout a simple single column, with no tables, text boxes, or graphics?
Applicant tracking systems misread tables and graphics, and your content silently disappears.
8. Does your contact line show city and province only, with no full street address?
City and province is all a Canadian employer needs, and all you should give.
9. Is it free of the line "References available upon request"?
Employers assume it. The line just spends space you need for results.
10. Do your bullets describe what you achieved, not just what you were responsible for?
"Responsible for X" is a duty. "Delivered X, which did Y" is a hire.
